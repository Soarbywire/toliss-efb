    Quick weather

    Applied immediately, for practice. Wind is set relative to the aircraft's current heading, so line up or set up on final first. Selecting quick weather switches off Real-World Weather.

    Wind
    Turbulence
    Visibility & cloud
    Wind shear Hits when you descend through about 1,000 ft above the field: a sudden headwind gust, then the headwind collapses into a 25 kt tailwind with turbulence for about 20 seconds; the previous wind then returns. ToLiss's reactive wind shear warning works from its own energy calculations, so it may or may not trigger; predictive wind shear (PWS) needs the weather radar and PWS switch on.

    Pushback

    Pushback is available on the ground only.
    The dashed line is the path of the aircraft's nose (hidden where it passes under the aircraft or the outline); the outline shows where the aircraft ends up. The push follows a towing model: the tug steers the nose wheel (within Airbus's 90° towing limit) and the aircraft pivots about its main gear. Once you start, the plan stays on the map: the travelled part turns solid. Check the path is clear: there is no collision detection.
    As with a real tug, the push starts when you release the parking brake, and stops if you set it during the push. You can start the engines during the push. The tug model isn't shown, and X-Plane may not animate the wheels while the EFB moves the aircraft.

    Replay IOS

    Mode
    ---
    These are X-Plane's own replay controls: X-Plane records the flight continuously and plays it back. Pressing a playback button enters replay if needed. Exit to Real-Time leaves replay and returns to the live flight where the aircraft actually is now (X-Plane does not resume flying from a rewound point). The EFB's Flight Recorder is separate and keeps recording. Replay is not available during a pushback or slew. Buttons this X-Plane version does not have are greyed out.

    TCAS Traffic IOS

    Traffic
    ---
    Intruders
    0
    Height above ground
    ---
    X-Plane TCAS
    ---
    Scenario
    IntruderScenarioRangeRel. altPositionCPASpeed / V/S
    The intruder flies a straight path planned to reach the closest point of approach after the time you set, using your present track, speed and climb or descent; it is removed a minute after it passes. Typical results: 0 nm and 0 ft in about 45–50 s gives a TA, then an RA. A larger miss distance or vertical separation gives a TA only. Several intruders at once make a multi-threat encounter.
    Needs: X-Plane 12.4.1 or later; the aircraft's TCAS in TA/RA. TCAS gives no RAs on the ground or below about 1,000 ft above the ground. Only one plugin can supply traffic: disconnect xPilot or turn off LiveTraffic first (the EFB gives the traffic back as soon as another plugin asks for it). The intruders appear on TCAS and the ND, not out of the window.

    Slew IOS

    Hold to move
    Keyboard: ↑ ↓ forward/back, ← → turn, Shift + ← → sideways, Page Up / Page Down climb/descend.
    Start slew, then tap the map to place the aircraft there.
    While slewing, X-Plane stops moving the aircraft and the EFB moves it: on the ground it slides along the terrain, in the air it keeps its altitude (never below 50 ft above the terrain). Ending slew hands control back: in the air at the resume airspeed, level; on the ground stopped. If X-Plane was paused, it is paused again.

    Setup
    Runway distances in landing data
    Get a free logon code at hoppie.nl/acars. Use the same code in the ToLiss ISCS (ATSU), with the aircraft callsign of your flight. The ops callsign is your choice: letters and digits, up to 8 characters, different from the aircraft. The code is stored only on this PC.
    Message display
    No messages yet.
    Send to aircraft sends from the ops centre; the message appears on the ToLiss MCDU (ATSU, AOC, RECEIVED MESSAGES). ToLiss's ATSU has no free-text page for company messages, so crew replies are sent here with Send as crew: they go from the aircraft's callsign to the ops centre, just as an MCDU message would. Messages are checked every 45 to 75 seconds, as Hoppie asks.

    Change stand

    The ops centre picks a different suitable stand: the airline's rule first, then stands it has used before, and so on. Among equally good stands the pick is random, so trying again gives another.
