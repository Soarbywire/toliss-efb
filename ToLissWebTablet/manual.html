Introduction

ToLiss EFB runs inside X-Plane as an XPPython3 plugin with its own small web server. You open it in any browser on your network, so an iPad, a tablet, a phone or a second monitor becomes your EFB, with nothing to install on the device.

The sidebar groups the pages the way a flight runs:

Preflight
Dispatch & OFP, Weight & Balance, Ground Services, Pushback.
Flight
Map & Telemetry, Airbus Checklist, MCDU, Cockpit Displays, Flight Recorder, Scratchpad.
IOS
The instructor station: Aircraft State, Slew, Reposition, Weather & Time, TCAS Traffic, Replay, Situations and Malfunctions.
Network
VATSIM Network, xPilot & ATC, and the Operations Centre.
Manual
This manual, at the bottom of the sidebar next to Settings. It also opens on its own at http://<EFB address>/manual.

Requirements

  • X-Plane 12. TCAS traffic training needs X-Plane 12.4.1 or later.
  • Any ToLiss Airbus: A319, A320neo, A321/A321neo, A330, A340-600.
  • The XPPython3 plugin, installed and working.
  • For the Operations Centre: a free Hoppie ACARS logon code from hoppie.nl/acars.
  • For dispatch: a SimBrief account (username or user ID).
Recommended ToLiss settingIn the ToLiss ISCS, enable X-Plane pause acts as freeze motion. The aircraft then holds still with its systems running while paused, which the IOS Freeze and the approach trainer rely on.

Installing and updating

First installation

  1. Check XPPython3.It must be installed and working in X-Plane 12.
  2. Download the latest releasefrom github.com/Soarbywire/toliss-efb/releases and unzip it.
  3. Copy two itemsinto X-Plane 12/Resources/plugins/PythonPlugins/: the file PI_ToLissWebTablet.py and the whole ToLissWebTablet folder.
  4. Start X-Plane(or choose Plugins → XPPython3 → Reload scripts) and load a ToLiss aircraft.
  5. Open the EFBin a browser: http://localhost:8080 on the same PC, or http://<your PC's local IP>:8080 from a tablet or phone on the same network.

On the first start the plugin builds two indexes in the background (airports and navigation data) and saves them in its folder. Later starts load them in under a second. They rebuild by themselves when your scenery or navigation data changes, for example after a Navigraph AIRAC update.

Port

The default port is 8080. If another program uses it, the plugin tries the next free port (8081, 8082 and so on). You can set the port in Settings, or in config.json in the ToLissWebTablet folder.

Updating

New versions appear under Settings → Updates and install in one tap, checked against the published SHA-256 checksum. Your settings, recordings, checklists, learned stands, CG learning and Ops Centre history are kept, and Undo last update restores the previous version.

After an update (or an undo), two steps finish it, in this order:

  1. In X-Plane: choose Plugins → XPPython3 → Reload scripts (or restart X-Plane). Until then X-Plane keeps running the old version.
  2. In the browser: reload the EFB page. Settings → Updates enables Reload page now as soon as the plugin is running the new version.

The EFB checks that the page and the plugin are the same version. If they are not (for example after copying a new version in by hand and doing only one of the two steps), a banner at the top says which step is left.

  • Updates install only while the aircraft is parked, and never while the flight recorder is recording.
  • Updating from 0.66 or earlier: install as usual, then choose Plugins → XPPython3 → Reload scripts. The EFB downloads the rest of the new version itself and shows its progress at the EFB address. When it says so, choose Reload scripts once more.

A flight, start to finish

A typical airline-style flight with the Operations Centre switched on. Each step links to the chapter that explains it.

  1. Load your SimBrief plan.Dispatch & OFP → Fetch Latest OFP (or let it load automatically). The Operations Centre sends the flight release, weather, NOTAMs and the preliminary load sheet to the MCDU, and takes your aircraft callsign from the plan.
  2. Load fuel and payload.Weight & Balance, or one tap from the OFP. The live CG and ZFW CG appear.
  3. Board.Open the doors in Ground Services (or ToLiss). When the doors are closed again and the payload is steady for a minute, the final load sheet and takeoff data arrive. Answer the load sheet on the DCDU.
  4. Push back.Pushback: plan it on the map, release the parking brake, and the tug pushes along the planned path.
  5. Taxi.Taxi routing gives turn-by-turn directions to the holding point, with your clearance if you type it.
  6. Fly.The Operations Centre records OOOI times and sends a departure report, ETA updates, fuel checks and destination weather.
  7. Arrive.At about 150 nm out: arrival ATIS, a stand assignment and landing data. After landing, taxi directions to your stand; on stand, a block-in summary.

Preflight

Dispatch & OFP

Enter your SimBrief username in Settings, then Fetch Latest OFP. The page shows the flight number and the ATC callsign separately, the route, fuel and weights, and the OFP PDF.

  • Takeoff and Landing performance sheets from SimBrief's runway analysis: V-speeds, flaps, FLEX, landing distances and margins. QNH is shown in the airport's own unit (hPa, or inHg where the METAR uses A2992), and runway distances in feet or metres as you choose.
  • One-tap loading of the planned fuel and payload into ToLiss.
  • Load automatically: tick Load my latest SimBrief flight plan automatically (off by default) and the plan loads when X-Plane starts and when the EFB opens.

Weight & Balance

Sliders for passengers, forward and aft cargo, and block fuel, applied straight into ToLiss. Under the fuel slider, the centre of gravity in % MAC:

CG
ToLiss's own CG with everything on board (the ISCS calls it GWCG). This is MACTOW on the load sheet.
ZFW CG
The same CG with the fuel taken out: the figure for INIT B / FUEL PRED. It does not move when you change fuel; it moves when passengers or cargo move.
Landing CG
The CG after the planned taxi and trip fuel is burnt, centre tank first, then the wings.
How the ZFW CG is foundThe EFB learns, once for each aircraft type, how each fuel tank moves ToLiss's CG, by watching the CG while the fuel changes with the same payload. The first few times you load fuel from this page it does so in three quick steps (a few seconds) to learn straight away. Until it has seen enough, the ZFW CG is marked estimate and the load sheet says EST.

Ground Services

External power, chocks, ground air, passenger and cargo doors, and de-icing. Each door button cycles CLOSE → AUTO → OPEN, using ToLiss's own door modes.

Pushback

A built-in tug; no other plugin is needed. Plan the push with sliders or typed values:

  • Straight back, then a turn with the tail left or right by any angle, then a final straight, at your chosen towing speed. Presets cover the common pushes, and Pull forward covers nose-out stands.
  • Towing model: the tug steers the nose wheel (Gentle, Normal or Tight, within Airbus's 90° towing limit) and the aircraft pivots about its main gear, using your aircraft's real wheelbase.
  • X-Plane's physics stay on: like Better Pushback, the EFB drives the aircraft along the path (speed and turn rate, correcting any drift) while X-Plane's gear keeps the wheels on the surface, so sloping or uneven aprons and add-on airport scenery are handled.
  • Map preview: the airport map (optionally satellite) shows the path of the nose and the aircraft at true size where it will end up.
  1. Plan the push and check the preview.
  2. Start pushback.The tug drives up and connects its towbar to the nose wheel first (about 8 s); then release the parking brake and the push begins, like a real tug. Pause/Resume and Stop are available throughout; Stop before the tug has connected cancels the push.
  3. Set the parking brake at the end. Until you do, the EFB holds the aircraft in place so idle thrust cannot move it.

Settings are remembered on every device; Reset returns to the defaults. Pushback is available on the ground only.

The tug. X-Plane's own towbar tractor (the GT110, with its driver) drives in from the side, turns to face the aircraft and creeps up until its towbar is on the nose wheel; nothing moves until it is connected. During the push it swings round with the nose wheel in the turn; it stays until you set the parking brake, then the towbar comes off and it drives away. While it is connected, ToLiss shows NWS DISC on the E/WD. Untick Show the pushback truck to push without it.

After Stop, and when a push has finished, the page goes straight back to planning from where the aircraft is.

CautionSetting the parking brake during the push stops it, as a real tug driver would. There is no collision detection: check the path is clear.

Flight pages

Map & Telemetry

A moving map with full airport detail from your installed scenery: taxiways, hold-short lines, taxiway signs and stands. Satellite view keeps the taxiway lines on top. Heading-up mode, follow, a tall-map option and freehand drawing are in the map tools.

The telemetry strip shows speeds, altitude, wind, QNH (in the airport's unit), autopilot, flaps, gear, fuel, transponder and the nearest taxiway or stand. Frequencies opens the radio stations of the airport you are at (see chapter 8).

Taxi routing

Map & Telemetry → Taxi

Directions to a stand or a runway holding point, along your scenery's taxi network: only taxiways wide enough for your aircraft, one-way rules respected, no U-turns, and runways only to cross them.

  • Choose To a stand or To a runway, or tap a stand on the map. Type your clearance as ATC gave it, for example B B4 A HOLD SHORT 16R, and the route follows it, or tells you which part is impossible.
  • The panel says where each suggestion comes from: planned in your SimBrief OFP, from the departure ATIS, assigned by the Ops Centre or chosen by you.
  • On the map: the route in magenta, hold-short points in red, the part already taxied faded. A strip shows the next step and its distance, the time to go, and SLOW cues before sharp turns and the stand. Leave the route and it plans a new one from where you are.
  • In flight, the panel shows your destination: choose your arrival stand and Save as my arrival stand.
Before departure
Optional tickbox: a dashed preview to the holding point once the plan is loaded; the live route starts when pushback ends or you begin to taxi. The runway is the departure ATIS runway (VATSIM) if it differs from the OFP.
After landing
Optional tickbox: the route to the Ops Centre's stand, or the one you chose.
NoteRouting needs an airport whose scenery includes a taxi network. Directions are advisory: ATC and your clearance come first.

Weather strip and EFB ATIS

Under the top bar, on every page

One line with the weather of the airport that matters now: the departure airport on the ground and while climbing out (within 40 nm), then your destination; on the ground anywhere else, the airport you are at. For example: YSSY DEP SIM · ATIS C EFB · RWY 34R · 340/15G23 · HW 15 XW 3L · Q1009 · 9KM CIG 2500 · 18°.

Real weather
When X-Plane downloads real weather, the strip uses the airport's METAR (tag METAR), for the destination too, from far away. If X-Plane is still blending in a new report and the sim's wind or QNH differs noticeably, the panel says what X-Plane is simulating.
Custom weather
When X-Plane's weather is set manually, or injected by another tool, the strip uses X-Plane's own weather (tag SIM) for both airports, with the temperature corrected for each airport's elevation: it is what you will fly in. The real METAR is still in the panel, for reference.
  • The coloured dot is the conditions: green VMC, blue marginal, red IFR, magenta low IFR.
  • The runway in use: while BeyondATC is connected, the runway it assigned you (from its flight set-up, then each clearance, taxi, takeoff or approach instruction that names one), else the runway its ATIS names; with a VATSIM ATIS (real weather), the one it names; otherwise your planned runway while its tailwind is 5 kt or less, else the runway most into wind. The wind is split into headwind or tailwind (HW/TW) and crosswind (XW) for that runway.
  • ATIS: from the ATIS source chosen in Settings → Weather strip (or in the strip's panel): Automatic (BeyondATC while it is connected, else a VATSIM controller's ATIS with real weather, else the EFB ATIS), BeyondATC, VATSIM or EFB ATIS. The same ATIS goes to the MCDU from the Ops Centre. If the chosen source has none for that airport, the automatic choice is used and the panel says so. Otherwise the EFB ATIS (marked EFB), built from the same weather as the strip: information letter, time, runway in use, wind, visibility, cloud, temperature and QNH. Its letter moves on like a real ATIS: with each new METAR (real weather), or when the custom weather changes enough to matter (wind, QNH, conditions), or when the runway in use changes.
  • Alerts appear only when something matters: ATIS D NEW when the letter changes (until you open the strip), crosswind from 20 kt, tailwind over 5 kt, thunderstorm, wind shear, hail, freezing precipitation, snow, CB, conditions below CAT I, and a runway in use that differs from your plan.
  • Tap the strip for the weather in full, the runway and wind components including the gusts, the ATIS text, the METAR and the TAF. Departure and Destination switch airports; Automatic goes back to the one that matters now.
  • Beside a taxi route the strip shortens to the ATIS letter and wind.
  • Show it Always, Only with an alert or Off: in the strip's panel or in Settings → Weather strip (kept on each device).

The Ops Centre sends the same ATIS to the MCDU (as EFB ATIS when it is the EFB's), and sends it again when its letter changes.

NoteX-Plane's own spoken ATIS cannot be read by plugins, so the EFB builds its own. The METAR and VATSIM ATIS come from VATSIM, the TAF from aviationweather.gov (or your OFP). Advisory only.

MCDU and Airbus Checklist

A live MCDU in Airbus colours with a keypad laid out like the real unit. Your computer keyboard types into the scratchpad as well. The Airbus Checklist reads your aircraft's ToLiss checklist, or one you upload in the same XML format.

Cockpit Displays

Live PFD with a sidestick indicator, the E/WD with ECAM messages, and the FCU with both EFIS panels.

The FCU and EFIS push buttons work: AP1, AP2, A/THR, LOC, APPR, EXPED, and both FD and LS. Each light shows ToLiss's own state, so it shows what the aircraft actually did. If a press changes nothing (the autopilot refuses to engage on the ground, for example), a short note under the FCU says so.

Flight Recorder and Scratchpad

The recorder logs more than 60 flight parameters and every ECAM message. Each landing gets a report: landing rate, g-load, touchdown distance from the threshold, centreline offset and stabilised-approach checks, with charts, a map track, and CSV or Google Earth (KML) export. The Scratchpad has typed notes and a drawing pad with coloured pens.

Instructor station (IOS)

The IOS pages are named and ordered like a Level D simulator's instructor station.

Aircraft State

Live altitude, height above ground, IAS, TAS, ground speed, heading, vertical speed, pitch, bank and fuel. In flight, set altitude, airspeed, heading, vertical speed, pitch and bank with immediate effect, one at a time or together (FL100, 250 kt, heading 270). Indicated airspeed is converted properly for the altitude and wind. Also: Freeze (X-Plane paused with ToLiss's systems running), altitude freeze, fuel freeze and sim rate ×1 to ×8.

Slew

Move the aircraft with the hold-to-move pad, the keyboard, or by tapping the map. On the ground it follows the terrain; Climb lifts it off, and descending to the lowest slew height (50 ft) puts it back on its wheels. Ending slew in the air hands control back at the airspeed you choose.

Camera: the same view buttons and move/zoom pad as on the Replay page, to watch the aircraft while you place it.

KeyMovement
↑ ↓Forward, back
← →Turn left, right
Shift + ← →Sideways
Page Up Page DownClimb, descend (at least about 500 ft/min)

Reposition and approach trainer

Move to any stand (nose wheel on the stop line) or runway at any airport. Stands that share a name in the scenery are all listed, labelled by size: 57 (C) and 57 (F). Quick positions place you lined up, on a 5 or 10 nm final, on a downwind, or overhead, with the FCU set.

The approach trainer offers any ILS, LOC, RNAV, RNP, VOR or NDB approach from X-Plane's navigation data (Navigraph included). Start on final, on an intercept, or at the start of the procedure. Optionally it programs the MCDU (approach, VIA, PERF APPR from the live METAR), activates the approach phase, sets the FCU and engages the autopilot with APPR armed.

Weather & Time

Live weather or manual presets, the time of day, and training weather applied at once:

  • Wind relative to your heading: headwind, crosswind either side, 25 kt gusting 35, tailwind, calm.
  • Turbulence none, light, moderate or severe; visibility down to CAT IIIA fog, or a clear sky.
  • Wind shear on approach: armed, it hits at about 1,000 ft: a headwind gust, then a 25 kt tailwind with turbulence. Your previous wind returns afterwards.

TCAS Traffic

Inject intruders for TA and RA practice (X-Plane 12.4.1 or later): head-on, crossing from either side, overtaking, climbing from below or descending from above. Set the time to the closest point of approach, the miss distance and the vertical offset, up to 8 intruders at once. They appear on TCAS and the ND. The EFB never takes traffic away from xPilot or LiveTraffic.

Replay

X-Plane's replay on the EFB: Start, Fast reverse, Reverse, Slow reverse, Pause, Slow forward, Play, Fast forward, End, and Exit to Real-Time. A REPLAY badge shows on every page while you are in replay.

Camera. X-Plane's views in one tap: Cockpit, Chase, Orbit, Tower, Runway, Fly-by, Moving spot and Free camera (only those your X-Plane has). Hold the arrow and zoom buttons to move the orbit, chase or free camera. Your Quick Looks are the views you saved in X-Plane; tick Name them to give them names (kept on each device). They work outside replay too.

Jump to the landing
Enters replay, rewinds to about 30 s before the last touchdown, switches to the runway view and pauses: press Play. X-Plane keeps only the last part of a flight in its replay.
Touchdown camera
In a replay, the runway view from 150 ft above the ground until the aircraft has slowed down on the runway, then back to the view you had.
Auto-cycle
In a replay, change view every 10, 20, 30 or 60 seconds through the views and Quick Looks you tick. The plugin runs it, so it carries on with the EFB page closed.

Situations and Malfunctions

Requires ToLiss Pro. ToLiss situations and the full failure list, plus one-tap malfunctions: engine failure at V1 (V1 from your SimBrief takeoff data), engine fire, engine failure after takeoff, hydraulic loss, generator and bleed faults, and cabin decompression.

Operations Centre

The EFB becomes your airline's ground station on the free Hoppie ACARS network and dispatches your flight to the ToLiss MCDU (ATSU → AOC → RECEIVED MESSAGES), from preflight to block-in. Every message is formatted for the MCDU's 24 columns.

Setting up

  1. Get a Hoppie logon code at hoppie.nl/acars and enter it in the Ops Centre setup and in the ToLiss ISCS (ATSU).
  2. Callsigns.Choose an ops callsign (for example QFAOPS). The aircraft callsign comes from your SimBrief plan automatically when Use the aircraft callsign from my SimBrief plan is ticked. It must match the flight number in ToLiss's INIT A.
  3. Save and connect.The status shows when the aircraft's ATSU is online. Messages wait while it is offline and go out when it is back.

What it sends, and when

The flight panel lists every message by phase, with an on/off switch and Send now for each.

PhaseMessageSent
PreflightFlight release, weather, NOTAMs, preliminary load sheetWhen the SimBrief plan is loaded
Final load sheet, takeoff dataWhen the doors close after boarding and the payload is steady for a minute (or at beacon on)
Departure ATISFrom VATSIM, again whenever the letter changes; the latest METAR if no ATIS is online
In flightDeparture reportAfter takeoff, with ETA and on-time status
ETA updates, fuel check, destination weatherWhen the ETA moves 10 minutes; if fuel at destination falls 500 kg below plan; on each new METAR in cruise
ArrivalArrival ATIS, stand assignment, landing dataAt about 150 nm or 30 minutes out
Block-in summaryWhen the parking brake is set on stand: block and flight times, fuel used, touchdown rate

Loading a different plan starts a clean message list: the previous flight's messages move to Previous flights (the last five are kept), and anything still waiting for the old plan is cancelled.

Load sheets and CG

The final load sheet is built from the actual load in the sim and awaits your acknowledgement. It gives the CG in % MAC:

MACZFW
Zero-fuel CG, for INIT B / FUEL PRED.
MACTOW
Takeoff CG, ToLiss's own figure.
MACLAW
Landing CG after the planned fuel burn.
EST
Shown until the EFB has confirmed the zero-fuel CG for this aircraft type (see Weight & Balance).
  • Last-minute changes after the final load sheet, before takeoff: an LMC message for a zero-fuel weight change of 100 kg or more; a revised final load sheet (next EDNO) for 500 kg or more.
  • Cautions are added to the takeoff data if the actual weight, the runway (the one BeyondATC assigned, or the ATIS from your chosen ATIS source) or the temperature differ from the plan.
  • Trigger: choose at beacon on in the setup if you prefer the final load sheet then.
  • Times in messages: sim time (the default) uses X-Plane's UTC clock, the one on the ToLiss clock and MCDU, for OOOI, the departure report, ETAs, progress, the load sheet and the block-in summary; real-world UTC uses your PC's clock. With sim time, early or late is measured against the time of day of the SimBrief schedule. Block and flight times are always the actual time elapsed.

DCDU

Ops Centre → Message display: DCDU, or the DCDU button on the MCDU page

Messages on an A320-family DCDU in the same colours as the EFB's MCDU and FCU, with response keys to suit each message:

MessageKeysResult
Final load sheetACCEPT / REJECTA rejection brings a revised load sheet
Stand assignmentROGER / REQ CHANGEREQ CHANGE gets another stand
QuestionsROGER / STBY / UNABLESent to the ops centre as the crew
Everything elseROGER

Also MSG−/MSG+, PGE−/PGE+, CLOSE and RECALL, PRINT onto a cockpit-printer strip, and BRT/DIM. A MSG light and a chime announce a new message. Responses go over Hoppie, so every device shows the same state.

Stands

The stand comes from your destination's scenery and suits your aircraft size and airline. Freight, maintenance and general-aviation stands are never assigned. The ops centre learns where your airline parks; change the stand at any time (automatically, or from a list with a map preview) and save it as a rule. Rules written as 57 cover every stand 57; 57 (C) covers just that one. The assigned stand is marked on the moving map, with a Route to stand button.

Requests from the aircraft

Send these as the crew and the ops centre answers automatically:

REQUEST WX YMML · REQUEST ATIS YSSY · REQUEST LOADSHEET · REQUEST TO DATA · REQUEST ETA · REQUEST STAND · REQUEST LANDING DATA · LOADSHEET RECEIVED

VATSIM and ATC

VATSIM Network

A live map with ATC sectors, RainViewer weather radar and a search for pilots and controllers. Tap an aircraft for a panel that updates while open: altitude as a flight level above about 10,000 ft (true altitude and QNH underneath), climb or descent rate, ground speed, heading and the filed cruise level and speed. VATSIM sends true altitude and ground speed, not indicated airspeed.

xPilot & ATC

xPilot connection status, COM radios, SELCAL alerts, and Nearby ATC: online controllers within 400 nm, with a green dot for those in VHF radio range. COM1 / COM2 set the standby frequency; swap it to active on the RMP.

Airport frequencies

The real-world stations of the nearest airport, your departure or your destination (ATIS, Delivery, Ground, Tower, Departure, Approach, CTAF), read from your scenery. A green dot and the callsign show a VATSIM controller online for that airport on that frequency. The list refreshes every 30 seconds while open.

BeyondATC

Settings → Pages → BeyondATC (off until you switch it on)

When BeyondATC runs on the same PC as X-Plane (Windows), the EFB reads its log live, %USERPROFILE%\AppData\LocalLow\Skirmish Mode Games, Inc\BeyondATC\Player.log (another place can be set in Settings), and shows:

  • The transcript: ATC's instructions to you, with the controller and frequency, and what BeyondATC understood you to say. Tick Show other aircraft for everyone else on the frequency. It scrolls by itself; scroll up to read back, and Jump to latest returns.
  • Your clearance, kept up to date from ATC's instructions: departure clearance, squawk (amber if your transponder differs), altitude, heading, speed, direct, the next frequency, expected and cleared approach, runway clearances and taxi route. New taxi clearances are planned on the map straight away (option Taxi clearances onto the map automatically, on by default), following the taxiways ATC named up to the holding point; Show on the map does the same and opens the map. Taxiway names are matched however the scenery labels them (C also matches C_1, TWY C or Charlie); a name missing from your scenery is skipped, with a note.
  • Radios and frequencies, updated live: COM1 and COM2 with the controller on each (or "no BeyondATC controller here"), your squawk and the next frequency; the airports BeyondATC is running (departure and arrival first, then nearby airports as it loads them) with their ATC frequencies from X-Plane's airport data; and the centre sectors it has brought in so far. A green dot marks a controller heard on your radio in the last 5 minutes, an open dot one BeyondATC has running. Tap a frequency to put it in COM1 standby.
  • BeyondATC's ATIS for each airport. While BeyondATC is connected, the weather strip and the Ops Centre use its ATIS, so the letter matches what its controllers expect, and the strip's runway is the one BeyondATC assigned you, with an amber alert if it differs from your plan.
  • The last instruction under the top bar on every page (Always, for 30 s after a new one, or Off: Settings → Pages). Tap it for the transcript.
NoteThis is BeyondATC's own debug log, not an official interface. The EFB only reads it and never affects BeyondATC. A BeyondATC update may change what can be shown; if the EFB no longer recognises the log, it says so instead of showing wrong data. The log starts again each time BeyondATC starts.
